# Role-lens strategy

## Why the breadth can help

Jason's experience genuinely spans support, customer engineering, integrations,
analytics, automation, software, and business communication. Hiding that breadth
would discard a real differentiator.

## Why the breadth can hurt

A homepage that claims equal readiness for Technical Account Management,
Customer Success Engineering, Data Analytics, Data Science, Application
Engineering, and Forward Deployed Engineering would make the professional
identity look unfocused and inflated.

## The solution

CareerOS uses one core identity and several role lenses.

A role lens:

1. uses the same underlying evidence
2. changes the ordering and interpretation for a recruiter
3. identifies current strengths
4. links to concrete projects
5. discloses important gaps
6. states what is not claimed
7. recommends one next proof artifact

## Role priority

### Immediate

- Senior Technical Support Engineer
- Technical Account Manager
- Customer Success Engineer
- Data Analytics

These roles can use substantial evidence already present today, though formal
title-specific gaps remain.

### Bridge

- Application Engineer

This is the active engineering development path. The decisive evidence will be
reviewed code, tests, deployment, and maintainable fixes.

### Long-term

- Forward Deployed Engineer

This is the convergence role combining customer ambiguity, implementation,
engineering, deployment, debugging, data, and outcomes.

### Exploratory

- Data Science

This page is useful because the Nerf turret and Python/SQL foundation are real.
It must remain exploratory until statistics, modeling, evaluation, reproducible
notebooks, and an end-to-end ML case study exist.

## Homepage rule

Never rotate the headline among role titles. Lead with the core identity, then
let recruiters choose the relevant lens.
